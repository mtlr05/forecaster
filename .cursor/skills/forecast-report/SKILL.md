# Forecast report

Write this report every time a forecast is produced, next to the forecast files (e.g. `forecasts/<TICKER>/report.md` and `report.pdf`). The forecast file stays the audit record; the report is the reader's document. Never change a forecast number in the report; any new arithmetic is marked "(derived)" and gets its own calculation tag.

The main report walks the base case. Bull and bear are a short value comparison in the valuation section. Their builds live in Appendix A.

## Voice
Sell-side analyst prose: professional and precise, but clear. In the summary, lead with the base-case number and name the cause. Use active voice for the forecast and for a judgment ("We forecast...", "We assume..."). A sentence that only reports the arithmetic takes the thing as its subject ("Procedure fees add $6.6 million"). Write "vs." not "versus", be objective and acknowledge risks. No internal method shorthand in the body (no coefficient names, ladder, step numbers, scale factors as decimals); explain judgments in words. The Estimate comparison section may use the names bottom-up, top-down, calibrated top-down, and Consensus. Every table and figure is introduced by a sentence saying what to take from it.

Bold is only the conclusion sentence of each `FORECAST UPDATE` bullet. The sentences under it, and the opening sentence of every other paragraph, stay in ordinary type. Do not bold a paragraph because it starts a section, a driver, or a risk.

Each supporting sentence does one job. A revenue path, a consensus anchor, a procedure build, and a fade are separate sentences. Outside the summary, tags stay on the sentence that holds the number. If one tagged sentence is split, repeat the cluster. Do not gather tags onto a single source line for the whole bullet. The summary does not use tags. The tag letter is the judgment mark. Do not add a star or a second flag beside an `S` tag.

Spell out a private name the first time the reader meets it (a lab, a drug, a pool term), in that sentence. Leave EBITDA, consensus, and the other ordinary finance words short.

A paragraph does the job of its section. The job is the one named in the structure list and in the heading. When a paragraph answers a different question, move it. The claims and their tags move with it. Do not drop a number, a judgment, or a tag to make the section shorter. Name a document in words the first time the body cites it. "The article" is not a name. The URL stays in Appendix B. Use a term only after that section has said what it refers to. A note that a source was not reopened is an Appendix B status. The body keeps the fact and the tag. It does not narrate the rewrite.

## Structure (in this order)
Title: `<Company> (<Exchange>: <Ticker>)`, subtitle `Company Update: FY<x>E–FY<y>E Revenue and EBITDA Forecast`, then an italic note with the date, currency and units, "forecast only: no rating, price target or valuation" unless a collaborative valuation exists (then say it includes a valuation but carries no rating), and a pointer to the forecast file and the numbered-sources appendix. Scenario rules do not go in that note.

A table in the main report is the base case across years, or a scenario summary limited to the headline metrics (revenue, EBITDA, margin, value per share). Line builds, volume and pool paths, valuation leaves, and year-by-year cash flows for bull and bear go to Appendix A.

1. **Estimate comparison.** **Figure 1 – Estimate comparison**, for revenue and EBITDA across the explicit years: bottom-up, top-down low and high (or the single point), calibrated top-down, Consensus, and the base for revenue and the base for EBITDA, which may differ. Show each other view divided by that metric's base, bottom-up divided by calibrated top-down, and bottom-up divided by Consensus. Ratios in the prose are words ("about 10% below Consensus"); the table may show them as 1-decimal percentages or factors. A missing view is "n/a" plus the short reason (no range, no estimate, guidance left). The numbers come only from `views` in the forecast file. Do not recompute them, except a displayed ratio already stored as `[C]`.
2. **Summary.** A `FORECAST UPDATE` header, then 3–4 `■` bullets on the base case. Bull and bear do not appear here. Each opens with a bold one-sentence conclusion that leads with the base-case number and names the cause, then one paragraph. No bracketed tags in the conclusion or the paragraph. The paragraph ends by naming the section, or the line subsection, that holds the detail. Which view is the base of revenue and which is the base of EBITDA is that pointer to Estimate comparison, not a tagged sentence after the bullets. Then **Figure 2 – Financial summary**, the base case: revenue, growth, gross margin, EBITDA, EBITDA growth and margin for 2 actual years plus the forecast years. The figure's source line keeps its tags.
3. **Business Description and Reporting Lines.** What the company does, then each reporting line and its key products: what each one is, where it's sold, how the company earns from it (direct sales, royalty, licence), and its share of revenue. **Figure 3** shows revenue by line for the last full year. Discuss each product still in development and any acquisition that recently closed, what line will hold it or if it will become its own business line. Cover what it is, the stated timing, any stated amount, and whether the explicit years already include it.
4. **Management.** The CEO's background, tenure and title, prior roles, ownership, and track record on capital allocation, deals and execution. Say plainly where the record is thin.
5. **Key Developments.** Dated recent events (deals, launches, approvals, guidance, financing, buybacks, legal) in **Figure 4**, any upcoming catalysts, plus a short commentary on what matters.
6. **Forecast.** The base case.
   - **How the evidence becomes the numbers.** Prose plus **Figure 5 – Evidence → judgements → forecast impact**, mapping each development, driver and risk to the base-case judgements it sets and its effect on the base-case numbers. Point back to Figure 1 for the four views, which view is the base of each metric, and any gap between the bottom-up build and that base. Name a one-time adjustment, or an incremental-margin or next-period-growth judgment, that rests on the history window. Name M&A costs treated as one-time, and say whether integration costs remain in the short-term cost base and when they come out.
   - **One subsection per reporting line.** Title is the line name. The base case. Volume vs. price where possible. A product still in development, or a recent acquisition, is discussed inside the line that will hold it. If Business Description says it will become its own business line, it is its own line subsection. Each line subsection opens with the cause, in one or two sentences. The cause is a dated event or a short sequence of them, a change already in the run rate, including a one-time that does not repeat, or the absence of a new event when the line continues. Name which it is. The first sentence may also name the mechanism, volume, price, or mix, when that is what the line is. If one subsection holds more than one cause, give each its own sentence. If Business Description or Key Developments already tells the event, name what sets the number and point there instead of repeating the dates. Then stay on volume, price, and the dollars. Do not invent an event so the opening has one. When a rank or another comparison sets a dollar band, the subsection states the band, whether the base and the bull meet it, and that the medium-term organic growth forecast is the other lines' trajectory plus one more year of that path. After the prose, a table. Columns and year-range splits match Figure 6, including a continued block when Figure 6 is split. Rows are revenue and growth, then any other input used to build that line's revenue (units, price, and the like). Add cost and margin rows only when that line was modelled explicitly. The table keeps the line name as its title. It is not given a new figure number, so Figure 5, Figure 6, and Figure 6a stay. A sentence before the table says what to take from it. An italic source line carries the tags. Use the number format below, and "May not sum due to rounding" when the rounded parts do not add up.
   - **Base case.** Say whether a product still in development falls inside the explicit window or in the years after it, and name the effect on next-period growth (gsnext) and incremental margin (mc), or say the pipeline was considered and did not move them. Then **Figure 6 – Base case by line**, with revenue by line, the total, growth, EBITDA and margin for the actual years (including the latest partial period if it's used) and each forecast year. Then **Figure 6a – Cash opex and margin bridge**, which the report always includes. It shows the pieces that build cash operating expense and the EBITDA and margin those pieces produce, the same path as Figure 6. Rows follow the build actually used: gross profit, each cash-opex component (core run rate, announced step-ups, launch or program costs, variable load), total cash opex, EBITDA, and margin. A sentence before the table says what to take from it. When an early year is built a different way, the sentence says so and the bridge starts at the first year the bridge covers. Cash and share adjustments are not written up in this section.
   - **Contingent payments.** Discuss a royalty, license, earn-out, or similar contingent payment whenever the filing or the call states an amount, a formula, or a trigger and the item is large enough to discuss. That discussion is this subsection. Cover what it is, who pays, what triggers it, the term, how it is earned, its share of revenue when known, and whether the base case includes the dollars. Name the line that holds the dollars. The subsection itself says whether the base case includes the dollars and names the line. Those two facts may also appear in the summary or the drivers. They are not left only there.
7. **Valuation (only for a collaborative run).** Skip this section, and keep the "no valuation" note in the title block, when no collaborative result exists. When one does, use only numbers from `valuation_result`. Do not recompute a value. Mark any arithmetic of your own "(derived)" with a C tag. Open with the result, not the run: one or two sentences give the base value per share against the price and the share of that value that is terminal. When the request has a post-run factor, show the raw and adjusted values side by side, tagged `[P]` with the user's date. One sentence gives the bull and bear values and points to Appendix A.
   - **Method.** In prose, in words: Finagle discounts free cash flow to equity at the cost of equity, takes the explicit revenue and EBITDA as given, extends them to its horizon using the starting margin, the incremental margin and the next-period growth fading to terminal growth, and adds a terminal value. Say how many years are explicit and how many Finagle extends, that terminal growth is Finagle's default (never set by us), and how negative cash flow is treated.
   - **Balance Sheet Adjustments.** This subsection comes after the method prose and before Figure 8. Its title is those words. Use only the worked schedule on the result. One sentence says year-0 cash and the current share count equal the opening figures plus the present values and the new shares. When the result says the stated cash or the stated share count already includes the items, the sentence says that figure is used as stated and is not added again. Then a table, one row per item: the name, the amount, the timing, the probability, the discount, the present value, and any shares issued. The table keeps the subsection title. It is not given a new figure number, so Figure 8 stays the valuation inputs. An italic source line carries the tags. An empty schedule is that one sentence and no table. Say that these dollars are outside EBITDA and are not in a later cash-flow year. Do not recompute a present value.
   - **Figure 8 – Valuation inputs, base case.** Cost of equity, cost of debt, tax rate, terminal growth, shares, price, debt and cash, starting margin, incremental margin (mc), next-period growth (gsnext), and Finagle's terminal WACC. Each row says whether it's a Finagle default, a forecast-file input or a judgment, and gets tags. The cash row and the shares row are the adjusted figures from Balance Sheet Adjustments. Explain in prose how each judgment input was chosen. A sentence before the table says what to take from it.
   - **Run record.** A note under Figure 8, tagged, and word for word where the result's own wording matters: every prompt input tagged `[P]` with its date, the defaults that were filled, sanity flags and validation warnings, the fetched market inputs, and the Finagle commit. Do not list raw field names in the prose. One sentence says what a warning means for the base case.
   - **Medium-term organic growth.** After the inputs are explained, the prose calls `gsnext` the medium-term organic growth forecast. One short paragraph says how the three leaves are set. `me` is the last explicit year's EBITDA divided by revenue. `mc` is the contribution margin, and the paragraph says whether it moved. The medium-term organic growth forecast is the next year from the forecaster's section 2f. A base-case figure follows. One row per line, then the continued path, then the total. Columns are the last explicit year, the next year, and the change. The changes sum to the dollar increase, and that increase divided by last-explicit revenue is the growth rate, marked derived with a C tag. A sentence before the figure says what to take from it. The figure uses the number format, caption, source line, and tags below. "May not sum due to rounding." It stays on one page. The bull bridge goes to Appendix A. A named bear, such as a renewal, stays a sentence and points to that appendix.
   - **Figure 9 – Value bridge, base case.** The present value of explicit-period cash flows, the terminal value and its share of the total, firm value, less debt, plus cash, equity value, and value per share vs. the current price (% upside or downside). A sentence before the table says what to take from it.
   - **Base-case year table.** After the bridge, the whole base-case Finagle statement, from the last actual year through the end of Finagle's horizon. Split it into more than one table when one table would not fit on a page. A sentence before it says the value per share is the result for the case and is not a row in the statement. Bull and bear year rows stay in Appendix A.
   - **Figure 12 – Sensitivity.** Include this figure only when the user asks for another run. Value per share when one input moves at a time (e.g. cost of equity ±1 pt, mc ±10 pts, gsnext ±5 pts). The numbers come from that run's result file.
   - **Figure 13 – Scenarios vs. price.** Bull, base and bear values per share against the price, with one sentence on what drives the spread.
   - **Caveats.** Name what the valuation leaves out or overstates: dilution or funding that is not in Balance Sheet Adjustments, including funding when cash goes negative, unweighted approval or event assumptions, dependence on the terminal value, financing terms not modelled, and anything else specific. The named items are already in opening cash and the share count. Draw no rating or price target unless asked.

8. **Risks.** Company-specific, then industry and regulatory, 2–3 sentences each, sized where possible. Where the bear is that downside, say so in those sentences and point to Appendix A for the build.

Appendices:
- **A – Bull and Bear Cases.** **Figure 7** shows the headline scenarios from the forecast file only (revenue, EBITDA, margin, and value per share when a valuation exists). Explain what differs and why, and label any story the forecast file doesn't support as qualitative. This appendix also holds the builds the body does not: bull and bear line builds, volume and pool paths, the three-case valuation inputs, the three-case value bridge, and **Figure 11** with the key rows for bull and bear. The base-case year table is in the Valuation section. It mirrors the "report" sheet of Finagle's Excel template (`company_template.xlsx`): a header block (ticker, price, shares, rates, terminal growth), then one column per year, with rows for revenue, EBITDA, SBC, interest, pre-tax income, NOL, cash taxes, capex, M&A, working capital, debt change, FCF, FCFE, FCFF, firm value, debt, EV, equity, cash, FCF per share, dividend, WACC and value per share. Add discount-factor and PV rows only if marked derived. Print Finagle's values as output; flag oddities (e.g. a sign flip) in a note instead of correcting them. A sentence introduces the sheet. Number any further appendix figures in the order a reader meets them, and do not reuse a body figure number. Use landscape pages in the PDF if a year-by-year table doesn't fit in portrait.
- **B – Numbered sources** (citation style below).
- **C – Method.** A short plain description of how the forecast was built (the forecaster skill's method). State that bottom-up, top-down, calibrated top-down, and Consensus are recorded and that revenue and EBITDA each have their own base, which may differ. State the history window in one or two sentences: at least nine reported quarters of filings (the financial reports, the earnings press releases, the call transcripts, and any shareholder letter), and from them revenue and EBITDA or margin where the company reports it. Reported results frame the forecast. The same history is the check for one-time items, the cost base, and the inputs past the explicit years. A year with a calibrated top-down uses that total. A year with no calibrated top-down uses Consensus when an estimate exists, and bottom-up when it does not. When a quarter has no call, say so, and the history window is not short for that reason alone. M&A-related costs are one-time unless the company says they continue. Integration costs stay only while the integration work is unfinished. The explicit window is the longest of two years, the guidance horizon, and the Consensus horizon, unless the prompt sets it. An update keeps the window already in the case unless the prompt changes it. An unlaunched product sits inside that window when its launch falls there, and otherwise in the years after it. It is not added on top of an explicit-year total that already includes it. Milestone payments the company owes, similar contingent cash outside EBITDA, and equity offerings are a schedule in the forecast file. A valuation discounts that schedule at the cost of equity and adjusts year-0 cash and the current share count. The worked figures are the Balance Sheet Adjustments subsection. They are not written up in the forecast section.

## Numbers
- Tables: US$M (or the reporting currency) to 1 decimal, whole numbers at $100M and above. Growth and margins to 1 decimal.
- When a converted display currency is shown (e.g. CPH in USD while consensus is in CAD), use the same cited conversion rate as the forecast file.
- Prose: about 3 significant figures for dollar amounts, whole percentages, margin changes in bps.
- Per share to 2 decimals, multiples to 1 decimal with an x.
- Years marked A or E (FY2025A, H2 2026E).
- Ratios and factors go in words ("about 10% below Consensus").
- Use the same figure in prose and tables. Add "May not sum due to rounding" under any table whose rounded parts don't add up.
- Full precision lives only in Appendix B and the forecast file.
- Every figure has a caption `**Figure N – Title**` and an italic `*Source: ...*` line with its tags.

## Citations
- **Inline:** every factual or numeric claim outside the summary ends with bracketed tags placed just before the full stop, e.g. `... converted from CAD [G1][E1][C3].` The summary states the base case and points to the section that carries the tags. No footnotes. The markdown keeps the brackets. The PDF raises a prose cluster to one superscript, as specified under PDF layout. Table cells and italic Source lines keep the brackets.
- **Tag letters:** A = actuals, G = guidance and management statements, E = estimates (consensus), S = assumptions and judgments, C = calculations, P = figures or instructions supplied in the prompt, D = documents. Reuse the forecast file's tags verbatim, and number new ones after them.
- **Appendix B, "Numbered sources":** grouped Documents → Actuals → Guidance and management statements → Estimates → Assumptions → Calculations → Not verified.
  - Document entries: **D#**, then publisher, title (contents), date, then a colon and the raw URL, with a note such as "(HTML; no page numbers)". URLs appear only here.
  - Fact entries: bold tag, the quoted source text, then the document and page (e.g. "D2, p.8"). Calculation entries give the formula with the input tags.
  - Anything without a verified link, page or date is marked "link unverified", "page unverified" or "date unknown" and listed under Not verified.

## PDF layout: no table breaks across pages
- Keep every table on one page together with its Figure caption, any sub-label and its Source line: wrap caption + table + Source in a block (e.g. `div.fig`) styled `break-inside: avoid; page-break-inside: avoid`, with `break-after: avoid` on the caption, `thead {display: table-header-group}` and `tr {break-inside: avoid}`.
- For tables too long for one page, split them into logical blocks (e.g. year ranges), each with its own label, and use a smaller font (about 6pt) for tables over about 24 rows, rather than letting a table break mid-way.
- After Markdown conversion, wrap each prose run of one or more bracketed tags that sits outside a table cell and outside an italic Source line in a single `sup`. Drop the brackets and join the tags with commas: `[G2][E1]` becomes `<sup>G2, E1</sup>`. Leave the tag characters as real text so a search still hits them. Style `sup` so the line spacing does not jump (`font-size: 0.75em; line-height: 0; vertical-align: super`).
- After rendering, check page by page that no table appears on two pages, and fix it before delivering.

## Finish
Render a PDF beside the markdown (e.g. with weasyprint, applying the layout rules above). Check that every forecast number matches the forecast file, that every valuation number matches the result file, that every claim outside the summary has a tag, that every tag resolves in Appendix B, that a main-report table is the base case or a headline scenario summary, and that no table breaks across pages. Then report the path and anything you couldn't source.
