# Driver patterns

Apply one pattern to a reported line, or add a line when the disclosure adds one. The pattern produces the unscaled build. Section 2c of the skill still scales that build to the total.

Pick the pattern management actually discusses. Then take the simplest one that fits the history. Do not import a pattern from a different industry because the product looks familiar. The examples below are illustrations of the pattern, not extra rules.

## Ramping

A line that is still being built: a recently launched product, a coverage win that is not yet in a full year, a region that just opened.

Use volume times price when you have both. Otherwise grow off the latest quarter and assume a slowdown, tagged `[S]`, because a new line rarely keeps its first-year growth. Watch a price change and a channel-inventory swing before you annualize a quarter. Inventory rebuild is not demand.

## New or acquired line

Management gave an amount or a floor (`more than $X`) and a start date. Phase it from the close or the launch. A floor is a floor, not a midpoint. Keep an acquired line out of the organic path if Finagle will book the deal in `acquisitions`.

## Mature or declining

Flat at the latest-quarter run rate, or the recent trend, plus only the structural changes management names (a lost contract, a discontinued SKU, a price reset). Do not mean-revert a decline just because it looks tired.

## Milestone-dependent

Revenue equals the event amount times a probability. The probability is `[S]` unless a source states it. Do not put the full amount in the base because the event would be nice.

## Acquisition-driven

Show organic revenue and the deal separately. Deal spend, year, and what the organic path excludes are the handoff to Finagle. Do not hide the deal inside a growth rate and also list it under `acquisitions`.

## Recurring

Starting base times retention, plus new bookings. Retention and the booking ramp are `[S]` or `[G]`, and they say whether the driver is customers kept or customers added.
