## 2. Business Description and Reporting Lines

Cipher is a specialty pharmaceutical company. It acquires products, takes them through development and approval, and sells them in Canada, the United States and South America [D11]. It reports one segment and breaks revenue into geography and type [A2]. We use three lines that add to the total: Natroba in the United States, the Canadian product portfolio, and U.S. licensing.

- **Natroba (U.S. product).** Spinosad, and its authorized generic, for head lice and scabies. Cipher bought the product from ParaPRO in July 2024 and sells it through its own U.S. channel [D13][A2]. Revenue is product sales, not a royalty.
- **Canadian product portfolio.** Epuris (isotretinoin) is the lead product, with Actikerall, Brinavess, Aggrastat, Vaniqa and Durela [A2]. Cipher sells these itself in Canada. Epuris is priced in Canadian dollars [S3].
- **U.S. licensing.** Royalties and product shipments on Absorica and Lipofen. Cipher keeps half of licensing revenue on the products covered by the Galephar agreement and pays the other half to Galephar, which reduces reported licensing revenue [D2].

## 3. Management

Craig Mull has been interim chief executive and chair since 29 July 2019, when he replaced Robert Tessarolo [D9][D7]. The company said then that it had started a search for a permanent chief executive [D9]. He is still interim [D2]. Before Cipher he was chief operating officer, vice president and director of corporate development at CML Healthcare from 1989 to 2004, and a sales and marketing manager at Baxter [D7]. He founded Typhon Group, a Toronto-area real estate developer, and holds an HBA from Ivey [D7][D8].

As of 2 May 2025 he controlled 10,384,697 common shares, 40.5% of the 25,622,940 shares then outstanding (derived) [D7][C15]. He takes no cash salary as interim chief executive. He is paid in shares for that role and in cash directors' fees as chair [D2].

The capital-allocation record we can see is the Natroba purchase, the debt paydown, and the buyback. Cipher drew $40 million in July 2024 to fund Natroba and had repaid all of it by 30 June 2026, including $5 million on 30 March 2026 [D2]. The prior normal-course issuer bid bought 532,940 shares for $5.4 million [D3]. A new bid, approved 1 May 2026, allows purchases of up to 1,490,343 shares through 4 May 2027. None were bought in the first half of 2026 [D2]. In the second quarter the company walked away from an acquisition it described as advanced [G5]. The record on a second large deal is still open.

## 5. Business Drivers by Line

**Natroba (59% of FY2025A).** This line is volume. The company attributes the 2026 decline to fewer patients covered by Medicaid, not to a price cut [A10]. Gross margin on the line was 86% for the first half and 84% in the second quarter [A9]. Management said expectations for the product have not changed and that performance should improve as the Walmart stocking program and a direct-to-patient channel take effect [G2]. No dollar cost for those programs was given. We do not rebound the second half to flat. We carry the first-half decline of 8.7% into the second half, and we allow only a low-single-digit recovery in FY2027E before scaling to consensus [S1][S2].

**Canadian portfolio (34%).** The driver is Epuris volume and mix. Market share of Epuris was 47.1% at 30 June 2026, down from 51.3%, but management said share had increased through the year to date [G3]. The second quarter grew 11.8% [S3]. Product-listing agreements were expected within 30 to 60 days of the 12 August call [G3]. We have no later filing that says they are signed. We hold the latest growth rate into the second half and use a 10% step in FY2027E for a first full year of broader public coverage [S3][S4]. The price is in Canadian dollars, and we assume the exchange rate stays at the rate used to convert consensus [S3][C3].

**U.S. licensing (7%).** Shipments to partners move around from quarter to quarter [G4]. The first half was up 2%, so we treat the second-quarter dip as timing and hold the second half flat at last year's second half [S5][A6]. FY2027E is down 10% before scaling, because Absorica's competitive position has already cut volumes and its market share was 3.4% [S6]. The Galephar split is already inside reported licensing revenue [D2].

## 6. Risks

**Medicaid volume.** Natroba's 2026 decline is a coverage loss, not a one-quarter shipment delay [A10]. If the covered population keeps shrinking, the low-single-digit FY2027E recovery does not happen. Natroba is 59% of FY2025A revenue [C16].

**Public-plan timing.** The Canadian step-up depends on provinces listing Epuris. The letter of intent does not force a province to list, and each plan can add conditions [D2]. A delay pushes the FY2027E Canadian number back toward the FY2026E run rate.

**Consensus gap.** The lines do not earn the consensus total on their own commentary. More than 10% of FY2026E second-half revenue, and a larger share of FY2027E, is the scale-up to the Street rather than a driver we can name [E1]. If the Street is high, both years are high.

**Sun award and Natroba in Canada.** The confirmed damages and the Absorica LD royalty are upside we do not model [D11]. So is a Health Canada approval of Natroba, which is only at the review stage [D2]. Either could lift revenue without a change in the U.S. Medicaid trend.

**Industry and regulatory.** Dermatitis and lice products face generic and branded competitors, and Absorica already shows that in a 3.4% share [S6]. Canadian public prices and U.S. Medicaid coverage are set outside the company. A minimum purchase of Natroba active ingredient is about $1.6 million in 2026 and about $2.5 million in 2027 [D2]. That is a cash commitment, not a revenue guarantee.

## 8. Valuation

Finagle values the shares at $10.08 in the base case [D12]. The bear case is $8.92 and the bull case is $11.53 [D12]. The dividend-discount value equals the free-cash-flow value in each case, $10.08, $8.92 and $11.53 [D12]. No prompt input was stated. The request's `prompt_inputs` list is empty, and `post_finagle` is null, so there is no adjusted value [D12].

Finagle filled these leaves as assumptions: `baseline.nol`, `baseline.noa`, `rates.re`, `rates.gt`, `rates.roict`, `rates.t`, `rates.rd`, `forecast.dwc.mode`, `debt.mode`, `debt.leverage`, `debt.start_year`, `distribution.mode` [D12]. The horizon is 10 years [D12]. There are no sanity flags [D12]. Validation warnings, in the result's own words: for the base case, "average EBITDA growth is 14.1% and there are no acquisitions. If that growth includes acquired EBITDA, use an organic path and book the purchase price with acquisitions, or MnA stays zero" and "forecast evidence mentions acquisitions but the case has none, so MnA stays zero" [D12]. The bear case has only the second of those [D12]. The bull case repeats the second, and says "average EBITDA growth is 25.0% and there are no acquisitions. If that growth includes acquired EBITDA, use an organic path and book the purchase price with acquisitions, or MnA stays zero" [D12]. The acquisition wording is the disclosure that the process did not close [G5]. MnA in the statement is zero [D12].

Fetched market inputs, all from Yahoo on 29 October 2026: price US$9.65, a risk-free rate of 5.045%, and 25.415 million shares [D12]. The unrounded price is 13.5 Canadian dollars times 0.714745 [D12]. Finagle commit `f00ccb27943c7e5d4fd8f4b4d41352060acbe216`. The run was at 5:50 p.m. Toronto time on 29 October 2026 [D12].

**Method.** Finagle discounts free cash flow to equity at the cost of equity. It takes the explicit revenue and EBITDA growth as given for two years (FY2026E and FY2027E), then extends that path for eight further years, through FY2035E, using the ending margin, the incremental margin and the following year's sales growth, fading toward terminal growth [D12]. The terminal value uses the free cash flow of the year after FY2035E [D12]. Terminal growth is Finagle's default, equal to the 10-year yield. We did not set it. Free cash flow to equity is positive in every year of all three cases, so the result has no negative-cash-flow year [D12].

## Appendix C – Method

We forecast only years the company has not reported. Year 0 is FY2025. The explicit window is FY2026 and FY2027, which is where the consensus and the call commentary stop. Lines are the revenue breakdown the company already reports. With no guidance range, the revenue total is consensus and the lines are scaled to it. EBITDA is built from the latest quarter's cash operating cost because no EBITDA estimate exists. Bear and bull move the same three levers: where revenue sits in the consensus range, which gross margin we use, and FY2027 cost inflation. The joint run copies those operating leaves into Finagle and adds nothing the request did not state. Finagle supplies the market price, the share count, the risk-free rate, the cost of equity, terminal growth, and the unstated horizon, tax rate, cost of debt, tax losses, non-operating assets, working capital and payout. The value per share comes only from Finagle.
