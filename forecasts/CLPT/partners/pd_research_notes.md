**Synthesis.**

- **Where the 10–15% comes from.** The figure traces back to an expert personal communication (Ontario 2005 [A1]) and a conference abstract [A2]. Aspen's S-1 repeats "10–15%" but uses it to mean the share of eligible patients who are *referred*, not the share of PD that is eligible [D1]. The figure is not supported by any measured study.
- **What the measured studies show.** They cluster at about **2–7% of PD at a point in time**:
  - Morgante: 1.6% strict, 4.5% flexible, measured in movement-disorder clinics, which already enrich for advanced patients.
  - D-DATS: 6.6% eligible for referral for any device-aided therapy, so DBS alone would be lower.
  - NHS England: 1–10%.
  - Literature summary [A5]: 4.5–10%.
- **Cumulative versus point prevalence.** The only US population-based figure is cumulative: about 5% of incident PD ever received DBS, with more planned among younger patients [A8]. A *lifetime* share of roughly 5–10% is plausible, which is probably what "become candidates" in [A1] meant. That is a different quantity from the point prevalence our pool uses.
- **Defensible figure for "all PD patients meeting a DBS-style label at a point in time": about 3–8%, central about 5%** (★ S, derived from A3–A8). On the evidence, the 10–15% is a lifetime or expert upper bound, not a point prevalence.
- **Our model sits inside this range.** The published-inputs model gives 4.9%, or 7.8% with an age cap of 80.

## Recommendation for the pool model (not yet applied)

1. **Drop the ×2.57 "target-matched" scaling as the base case.** Re-base the calibration target to about 5% (range 3–8%) point prevalence. Keep 10–15% only as a labelled cumulative or upper-bound sensitivity. The published-inputs case (4.9%) then becomes the base, needing a gap factor of about 1.0 (★).
2. **Fix the real calibration gap, which is willingness and uptake, not pool size.** With a 4.9% pool, DBS under the cap (about 3.4k/yr) exceeds willing × covered inflow (about 2.6k/yr). The 35–43% "willingness" figures measure consent to referral or general attitude [A11], not uptake among fully eligible patients. Population data show 29–36% of patients with fluctuations actually implanted, with DBS planned for about 78% of the remaining younger patients [A8]. **Re-specify willingness as about 50–70% among label-eligible candidates (★, to test).** Alternatively, check whether the 3,990 DBS count includes over-75s, revisions or battery changes, or non-PD indications [A10 note].
3. **Expected effect.** Class demand moves toward the published-inputs bounds, scaled up by the higher willingness:
   - Rough FY2040 estimate (★, C): if the one-time therapy replaces DBS, about 3.1k × (0.6/0.391) ≈ 4.8k/yr. If DBS keeps today's volume, the result is small and positive. This needs a model rerun.
   - Either way, this is about half of the 9,200 partner sum. That argues for lowering the partners.md PD figures or tying them to class demand.
4. **Present the partner volumes as unguided judgment.** No company supports them. For Aspen, cross-check against its stated "hundreds per module".

## Part 3. Separating the funnel stages (added 24 Oct 2026)

*David's point: the "candidate" figures sit at different stages of the funnel. The stages are:*

1. *Medically eligible, by clinical criteria*
2. *Referred or evaluated*
3. *Willing*
4. *Covered or able to get access*
5. *Treated*

*Each stage is a subset of the one before it, so treated = eligible × referred × willing × covered. Treated counts are therefore only lower bounds on eligibility. Tags and read levels are the same as above. New sources are numbered B1 onward.*

### 3.2 The actual clinical criteria, and patients who are not refractory to DRT

- **FDA label, Medtronic (Activa/Percept/SenSight)** [B1]: "levodopa-responsive Parkinson's disease of at least 4 years' duration that are not adequately controlled with medication, including motor complications of recent onset (from 4 months to 3 years) or motor complications of longer-standing duration."
  - There is **no age limit**, and the label does not require patients to be *refractory* to DRT.
  - "Not adequately controlled" together with "recent onset" covers EARLYSTIM-type patients.
  - Safety is not established in dementia, moderate-to-severe depression, under-18s, coagulopathy, or prior ablation.
  - Read level: FT of the FDA approval letter / summary of safety and effectiveness (SSED) text.
- **Abbott Infinity/Liberta**: "advanced levodopa-responsive Parkinson's disease that are not adequately controlled by medications". No duration requirement [B2]. FT (Abbott HCP page).
- **Boston Scientific Vercise**: "moderate-to-advanced levodopa-responsive PD inadequately controlled with medication" [B3]. Snip (payer policy quoting the FDA approval).
- **CAPSIT-PD (1999)**: idiopathic PD, at least 5 years' duration, and at least 33% UPDRS-III improvement on levodopa challenge, plus neuropsychological work-up [B4, B5]. CAPSIT-PD itself sets no age cap. In practice centres use about 70–75 [B6].
  - Read level: FT of Artusi et al. 2020 review [B5]; Defer 1999 itself not read.
- **EARLYSTIM (Schüpbach, NEJM 2013)** [B7]. FT (methods):
  - Inclusion: age 18–60; at least 4 years' duration; H&Y below 3 when on medication; at least 50% levodopa response; fluctuations or dyskinesia for no more than 3 years; and UPDRS-II above 6 in the worst condition, or SOFAS 51–80% (functional or social impairment).
  - Exclusion: dementia (Mattis ≤130), severe depression, psychosis.
  - 251 of 392 screened were enrolled, but screening was of referred patients, so this is not a population ratio.
  - The trial shows patients with **early, non-refractory** complications benefit. It is the basis of the "recent onset" wording on the label.
- **Expert guidance** (Canadian J Neurol Sci 2016 [B6]): no formal age cutoff; most ideal candidates are under 70; dementia is an absolute contraindication; MCI is unclear. Severe tremor qualifies even when it responds poorly to levodopa. Snip and abstract-level.
- **Not found**: AAN or MDS guidelines that give numeric eligibility criteria. I also found no published study that applies the label or EARLYSTIM criteria to a **population-based** cohort. Morgante (clinic, CAPSIT-based) and D-DATS (general hospital, any device therapy) are the closest. **This is the key evidence gap.**

**Implication for the model.** The label requires motor complications that are not adequately controlled, not refractoriness. Our pool already takes this approach: all patients with motor complications × 70% "disabling" × levodopa-responsive × fit, with no duration window. "Disabling" (S1) is therefore the stage-1 judgment that matters, and refractoriness should not enter. Aspen's "refractory to DRT" envelope (300k) is a looser and different concept.

### 3.4 Downstream rates implied by about 3,990 DBS a year

**What the 3,990 counts** [A10, Venkatraman 2024, FT table]: weighted National Inpatient Sample (NIS) discharges in 2019 with a PD diagnosis and a DBS procedure code. So it is:

- PD only (essential tremor is counted separately at 1,995);
- all ages;
- **inpatient only**. Outpatient or same-day implants are missing, so true volume is likely higher (★, unquantified).

It may also include staged second-side procedures, so the count is not purely patients (★). The share aged under 75 is not reported; S5 = 85% is unverified. The model's under-75 figure is about 3.4k a year.

**Arithmetic (C, using the cohort model's published case).**

- **Definition (b) at 4.9%.** The model has about 8.3k patients a year newly becoming eligible under 75, and a stock of 44.9k.
  - Lifetime treated fraction of eligible patients ≈ 3.4k / 8.3k ≈ **41%**. This is roughly referral × willing × covered over a lifetime, ignoring exits before treatment, which would push the true figure higher.
  - Cross-check: Olmsted found 29% of all fluctuators ever had DBS. The eligible group is a subset of fluctuators, so 41% among eligible patients is plausible [A8].
- **Definition (b) at 8%.** Eligible inflow is about 13.6k a year, so the product ≈ **25%**.
- **Definition (c) at 15%.** Eligible inflow is about 25k a year, so the product ≈ **13%**.
- **Definition (a) at 1.5%.** The product is above 100%. This is impossible, so the strict definition **cannot** be the population DBS draws from: surgeons operate outside strict CAPSIT.

**Comparison with the literature on the downstream stages.**

- **Referral and evaluation**:
  - For 26.6% of untreated advanced patients in Spain, a device therapy was "not considered" [A7].
  - Aspen asserts that only 10–15% of eligible patients are referred [D1, unsourced].
  - In Olmsted, DBS was planned for 78% of untreated young patients with fluctuations [A8].
  - Range: about 30–80%.
- **Willingness**:
  - 43.2% consented to referral [A11]; 35% [A11 of the earlier notes].
  - Spain: 13.3% of untreated advanced patients rejected a device therapy, implying higher acceptance among those offered.
  - Range: about 40–85%.
- **Coverage**: Medicare covers DBS under the label, so access is the main constraint rather than coverage. Range: about 80–95% (★).

**Product of those ranges.** About 0.3 × 0.4 × 0.8 ≈ 10% at the low end, and 0.8 × 0.85 × 0.95 ≈ 65% at the high end. Central about 0.55 × 0.6 × 0.9 ≈ 30%.

That central figure is consistent with definition (b) at about 6–7% eligibility, which needs a product of about 30%. Definition (c) at 15% needs about 13%, the low end of plausible. **The 35–43% figures are willingness to be referred (stages 2–3), not the whole downstream product.** Using them as the only factor and assuming everyone is referred, as the current model does with will = 39%, cov = 80% and implied k = 100%, double-counts nothing but leaves out referral. That is why the published case cannot reach 3.4k.

### 3.5 Recommendation (not applied)

1. **Make every calibration target stage-specific.**
   - Stage 1 target: about 5% (range 3.5–8%) point prevalence under definition (b), from Morgante flexible, D-DATS and the cohort model. Show (a) and (c) as bounds. Stop calibrating to 10–15%.
   - Stage 5 target: treated patients a year = 3,990 (inpatient PD DBS, 2019; note the outpatient undercount), plus Olmsted ever-DBS ≈ 29% of fluctuators as a lifetime check.
   - Downstream: model referral (r), willingness (w) and coverage (c) as **separate** inputs, with ranges r 30–80%, w 40–85%, c 80–95%.
   - Calibrate the product r·w·c (about 25–45% lifetime) so that eligible × r·w·c reproduces DBS volume. Do not scale stage 1 up.
2. **Drop the ×2.57 target-matched case.** Replace it with the stage-consistent case above. It raises the willing pool through w and r, not through eligibility. For a one-time therapy, referral and willingness could plausibly be higher than for DBS (no implanted hardware). Make that an explicit scenario (★), not a hidden scaling.
3. **Aspen's 150k.** Treat it as a company-sourced upper-envelope point (about 15% of PD), in the same class as definition (c). It is not evidence for the stage-1 DBS-label pool. It also embeds "refractory to DRT", which the label does not require. Cite it only as "company belief, unsourced". Keep it separate from Aspen's genetic-PD 150k.

**Synthesis of the share under 75.**
- E2 is the only US source with age bands for new procedures in the elderly: 28% of new DBS in patients ≥65 were 75 or older.
- Combining E2 with the share of PD DBS patients aged 65 or over:
  - 49.6% in NIS 2006–10 (E3); 33% in MarketScan, which is skewed young (E1).
  - The median age of 66 for all 2019 DBS (E5) points to about 50–60% for 2019 ★.
- That gives 0.50–0.60 × 28% = 14–17% aged ≥75, so **83–86% under 75** (C ★).
- This agrees with the normal-distribution reading of NIS (87%, E4). The bounds are:
  - one US centre in 2013–19 (80.5%, E6), which leans older because it published on elderly DBS;
  - MarketScan (93%, E1), which leans younger.
- **Conclusion: 85% is supported as a central figure. Plausible range 80–90%** (★; there is no direct 2019 US table by age for PD DBS).
- Caveats:
  - E2's age mix is from 2007–09, and the share of patients 75 or older may have risen since then; E1 reports no rise through 2009.
  - E2 reports that 18.5% of new Medicare DBS recipients had a dementia or cognitive-impairment code. Some treated patients are therefore outside the strict eligibility definition, a further caution on calibrating to treated counts.

**Implied under-75 DBS count (2019, before adjustments).** 3,990 × 85% = **3,392**, with a range of 3,192 (80%) to 3,591 (90%) (C).

### 4.2 Does the 3,990 undercount (outpatient surgery) or double count (staged implants)?

- **Corroboration.** A separate NIS analysis finds 3,945 DBS-lead hospitalizations in 2019 with PD as the *principal* diagnosis (E5). Venkatraman's 3,990 counts any PD diagnosis, so the two agree within 1%. Both are weighted national estimates; E5 gives a 95% CI of ±13% for all DBS (7,625; 6,664–8,586) (A).
- **Outpatient.** E5 states that the NIS "does not include ambulatory surgical facilities that may offer DBS implantation, or day surgeries that do not involve hospital admission. As DBS can be performed in these settings, we are unable to comment on the absolute number" (A).
  - **I found no national figure for the outpatient share of DBS lead implants in 2019.**
  - What exists:
    - one centre's 44 outpatient vs 70 inpatient cases over 2018–22, after a COVID-driven switch (Neurosurg Oper 2023; abstract);
    - NIS median stay of 1 day;
    - MarketScan, 2000–09: over 98.5% stayed one day (E1).
  - Medicare's inpatient-only status for CPT 61863/61867 in 2019 was **not verified**. The manufacturer reimbursement guide did not parse.
  - Judgment: in 2019 the outpatient or day-surgery share was probably small, about **0–15%** ★, because outpatient DBS spread mainly after 2020. That means an uplift of ×1.00–1.18.
- **Double counting.** The same NIS paper states that "if a patient was admitted for a staged implantation (i.e., electrodes placed in one brain hemisphere followed by the other at a later time point), that patient may appear more than once" (A).
  - Separate admissions for the generator (IPG) do not count, provided the count is lead-coded. The VA/Medicare cohort's 58.9% "staged" figure is electrode vs generator (PMC6592829), so it is not the relevant rate.
  - The share of US PD patients with bilateral leads placed in two separate admissions is **not published nationally**.
  - Single-centre series show staged bilateral is common at some centres (a matched study of 252 staged vs 252 simultaneous, Neurosurgery 2025; Cleveland Clinic under-70 comparison, PMC4724316).
  - Judgment: 0–25% of patients are staged with both stages inpatient ★. Patients = discharges ÷ (1 + staged share), a factor of ×0.80–1.00.
  - Some patients are unilateral only; this does not double count.
- **Net.** The two adjustments roughly offset: about ×0.80–1.18, central about ×1.0 ★. **There is no case for moving the central 3,990.** The range should be widened instead.
