## 3. Business Description and Reporting Lines

Pro-Dex designs and manufactures autoclavable, battery-powered surgical drivers and shavers for orthopaedic, thoracic and craniomaxillofacial (CMF) device companies, mostly as a contract or co-development manufacturer, and since February 2026 also machines parts for aerospace and defence through Advanced Precision Machining (APM) [D1]. It reports one segment; we use its revenue-by-type disclosure as the lines [D1]. Non-recurring engineering (NRE) means billable customer-development work [D1]. ### Monogram / Zimmer Biomet: hardware, sourced-product fees and cash milestones

Zimmer completed its acquisition of Monogram on 7 October 2025; PDEX remains an independent supplier, not a Zimmer subsidiary [A37][D23][D18]. The operative 3 October 2023 supply contract, unlike the ineffective 2018 agreement, separates exclusive PDEX-manufactured end effectors and gearing from sourced burrs, saw blades and custom patient-specific implants [G16][G15]. Hardware uses cost-based pricing with a redacted target gross margin and annual adjustment; development services are time-and-materials [G16][D18]. The sourced-product fee is a redacted percentage of Monogram’s eligible purchases, payable on purchase/payment timing, not a disclosed fixed amount per completed operation; the robotic arm and navigation cameras are excluded from that basket [G15]. PDEX has no manufacturing responsibility for these sourced items, supporting a potentially capital-light fee stream, but neither a near-100% gross margin nor an EBITDA margin is disclosed [D18][S22].

The latest PDEX 10-Q expects validation units in the second half of FY27 (January–June 2027), not the October–December 2026 start in Guillaume Meignin's article of 29 June 2026 [G10][D17]. In June 2026 the CEO said Monogram “could definitely be customer number two” within a couple of years, but declined to quantify revenue [G17]. On 4 November, Zimmer’s CEO anticipated a surgeon-guided fully autonomous launch in calendar H1 2027, pending regulatory clearance; that statement is not a PDEX purchase order, and a 510(k) filing is not clearance [G18]. We do not map installations of ROSA, Zimmer’s existing robotic-surgery platform, or all Zimmer robotic procedures into PDEX-eligible fee volumes [D23][G15].

Investment proceeds and contingent value rights (CVRs) are separate from product sales. PDEX received $8.9M for its Monogram equity and a $2.3M first milestone; the gains sit below operating income and are excluded from EBITDA [A37][A38]. Remaining contingent value rights (CVRs), which pay on specified milestones, are uncertain and cannot be added as recurring operating sales or counted again as cash already received [D1][D2].

The base case includes $0.307M/$6.2M of Monogram revenue [S23][S24][S25][S26]. That revenue sits within the base total, not on top of it [D23][S28]. Validation and commercial kits are recorded in medical devices [S24][S28]. Billable engineering stays in non-recurring engineering and prototype services [S26][S28]. The sourced-product fee is provisionally assigned to discounts and other operating revenue, because its final reported presentation is unavailable [S26][S28].

The supply term begins after ten end effectors have been developed and validated and runs for fifteen years; this is a contractual trigger, not a guaranteed ten-unit annual order [D18][D25]. Monogram pays the sourced-product fee as a percentage of gross eligible unit purchase cost multiplied by purchased units [G15]. Fee percentage, eligible purchase mix and timing are not disclosed, so the historical fee share is unavailable and the modeled base contribution remains an assumption [S25]. Ordinary Persona implants are not automatically custom patient-specific implants [D25].

### Monogram hardware, engineering and purchase fees

Commercial kits and purchase fees wait on regulatory clearance of Zimmer Biomet's surgeon-guided fully autonomous system, which has not occurred [G18]. Validation shipments are the earlier PDEX step, the dollars below are the weighted shipment plan, and the Monogram subsection and Figure 4 give the dates [G10][S23][G18].

The manufactured products are autoclavable bone-milling end effectors and gearing that converts rotation into sagittal motion [G16][D25]. Routine functional testing belongs in unit manufacturing cost, not a separate engineering invoice [D25]. We record these validation and commercial kits in medical devices [S24][S28]. Billable engineering stays in non-recurring engineering (NRE) and prototype services, while the sourced-product fee is provisionally assigned to discounts/other operating revenue because its final reported presentation is unavailable [S26][S28].

We assume commercial delivery starts in April–June 2027, after validation during January–June [S23]. FY27 keeps ten conditional systems at 40% realization, four expected systems [S23]. FY28 conditional systems are 348 at 70% realization, 244 expected systems, because that is the count that brings Monogram revenue to the $6.20M second-customer band at the existing kit price [S23][G17][A43]. The old plan of 120 systems was not a cap the company stated [S23]. These weights are timing and adoption judgment, not clinical-trial success probabilities [S23]. Expected values can contain fractional kit or procedure equivalents; they are not literal fractional purchase orders [S23][S24][S25].

We assume four end-effector/gearing kits per new system for working, backup and sterilization rotation [S24]. A $6,000 kit price is supported only by a cost sketch of $2,500 materials, ten direct hours at $70 and roughly $850 indirect/testing cost [S24]. The modeled hardware gross margin approximates the trailing company margin; neither the bill of materials nor the price is a supplier quotation [S24][C4]. Spares equal five percent of beginning installed kits, with no current-year kit replacement assumption [S24]. The one-year warranty and multi-year handpiece life do not support an immediate billable repair annuity [D18][D21].

## 7. Risks

Customer concentration is the largest company-specific exposure: one customer accounts for 78 percent of FY26 sales and its contract runs through calendar 2028 [A36][G3]. The Zimmer/Monogram supplier evidence does not identify that customer [D18][D26]. Appendix A combines core-customer volume stress with a delayed robotics launch rather than assuming robotics immediately offsets the exposure [S16][S23].

Supplier and repair risks remain operational rather than purely cyclical [G5][G19]. A finish-supplier delay can shift shipments, while labor under-utilization and replacement-part cost weaken repair margins [G5][G19]. These pressures are reflected in the downside build in Appendix A [S16][S19].

The regulatory and adoption sequence is tight because PDEX's validation window overlaps Zimmer's targeted launch [G10][G18]. Clearance, sterilization validation, surgeon training, hospital capital budgets and utilization can delay hardware purchases and fee revenue [D18][S23]. The bear in Appendix A assumes no commercial launch through FY28 [S23].

Kit counts, price, fee eligibility and the redacted percentage are not verified economics [D25][S24][S25]. We exclude a broad custom-implant fee opportunity unless its eligibility and purchase base are demonstrated [S25]. A warranty burden, incremental tooling or greater sustaining engineering could reduce contribution below the selected cost bands [D18][D2][S26].
